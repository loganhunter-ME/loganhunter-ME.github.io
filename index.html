About

I'm headed to the University of Colorado Boulder this fall to study mechanical engineering, with my sights set on aerospace. That interest was sharpened during a selective two-week fellowship with the Colorado Space Business Roundtable, which included site visits to Lockheed Martin, BAE Systems, and others, including direct conversations with engineers about how aerospace hardware actually gets built, designed, and tested.

Most of what I know about engineering, though, came from my three years on Team Impulse 4418 — a FIRST Robotics Competition team where I served as Team Captain & Project Team Manager. I spent those years directing a 35-plus-member engineering organization, sitting in design reviews, and working directly for designes on drivetrains, indexers, shooters, and more under real competition deadlines.

Outside FRC, I co-founded my school's chapter of the National Technical Honors Society and competed on a second robotics team, BEST Robotics, in strategy and General Engineering Management roles.

FEATURED ROBOTS

DWG NO. FRC-2026 · REBUILT

SPINO

~115 lb · ~14 ft/s top speed

Our 2026 competition robot, built to intake large volumes of "fuel" (foam dodgeballs) and shoot them on a parabolic trajectory for efficient scoring. Four major systems came together to make it work: a shooter with a vision-integrated hood that automatically adjusts trajectory to score from multiple distances, a swerve-module chassis for speed and responsiveness at top speed, a central indexer that funnels fuel into a single feed point, and a large frontal intake built to collect fuel at a high rate. (CAD shown with the robot's fuel-containment walls removed for visual clarity.)

  • ShooterVision-integrated hood, auto-adjusting trajectory
  • DrivetrainSwerve · ~14 ft/s
  • IndexerCentralized funnel to shooter
  • IntakeHigh-rate frontal fuel collection
DWG NO. FRC-2025 · REEFSCAPE

NESSIE

~125 lb · ~13 ft/s · instant turning

Our 2025 competition robot, built around scoring "coral" onto a large reef structure. A swerve chassis gave it a ~13 ft/s top speed with near-instant turning, while a large central funnel directed coral into the main mechanism and an end effector placed it precisely on the reef. A climbing mechanism — using barbs paired with a high gear-reduction motor — could lift the entire robot into the air for additional points at match end.

  • DrivetrainSwerve · ~13 ft/s · instant turning
  • FunnelDirects coral into main mechanism
  • End EffectorPlaces coral on the reef to score
  • ClimbBarb + high-reduction motor lift
DWG NO. FRC-2024 · CRESCENDO

BRONTO

~115 lb

Our 2024 competition robot, built around a flywheel shooter mounted on a lever arm to launch "notes" and score points. A large, non-backdriveable worm-gear box lifted the shooting mechanism to fire from multiple positions on the field. BRONTO also marked the team's first competitive use of swerve drive, a step up in speed, efficiency, and responsiveness for the drive team that we've built on ever since.

  • ShooterLever-arm mounted, flywheel-driven
  • LiftNon-backdriveable worm gearbox
  • DrivetrainSwerve — team's first competitive use
